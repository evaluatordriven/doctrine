## 1. Why does Evaluator-Driven Iteration (E-DI) exist?

**E-DI exists so delegation leads to quality.**

Quality here means outputs that are **fit for purpose** and **worthy of the craft** — not a single generative pass accepted because it was cheap or fluent.

**Corpus anchor**

- README: E-DI is a **delegation doctrine** — *how quality survives delegation when the person accountable for the work is not the person doing it.*
- Mandate **P1–P3** (principal): poor human-to-human (and now human-to-agent) delegation; need valuable, fit-for-purpose outputs; E-DI is early identification of misalignment corrected through consistent evaluation, actionable feedback, resourceful generation, and well-managed iteration.
- Doctrine: generation is near-free; the binding constraint is **evaluation design**.

---

## 2. How does E-DI behave?

- **Roles are different specialties.** Orchestrating, making, and evaluating are distinct. Each role that runs as a program or model gets **its own engine** (or a declared double-hat on the record). The Maker never grades its own work; the Evaluator never produces the artifact; the Orchestrator does not impersonate either.
- **Clarify “done” and “good” before making or evaluating.** Definition written after seeing a draft is an anchor on the draft (Mandate **M1.1**).
- **The system works as one loop.** Each role gets the awareness, resources, and opportunity to improve the **next** iteration — feedback must be able to change the next generation, or the loop is theater (Mandate **M3.6**, **M4**).
- **Every path has a defined exit** — terminal states `accepted` · `needs_owner` · `rejected` (and a recorded **outcome** beside the state) (Mandate **M5.2**; Contracts §2; Instrument Exits).
- **The Owner defines values, priorities, voice, and risk appetite** (Mandate **X1–X3**). E-DI does not.
- **Verdicts and Owner decisions stay distinct.** The loop’s verdict stands as produced. The Owner may accept what the doctrine would reject (or reject what it would accept) only as a **disposition recorded beside the verdict — never as a rewrite of it** (Mandate **X4**).

**Note on “researching”:** Research is a **mode of work** (often ladder **L2 — research and report**, Mandate **M8**), not a fourth standing seat beside Maker/Evaluator/Orchestrator. When research is delegated as its own job, it is still a delegation job on the sheet (Buyer, standard, Evaluator, exits) — not a silent fourth engine inside every loop.

## 3. What does E-DI do?

**E-DI operates delegation jobs that produce outputs** (documents, software, media, specs, and other artifacts).

**Operating shape (thin)**

- **One yardstick per job:** one artifact, one pass rule, one record, one terminal state (Instrument sheet).
- **Entry A (commissioned):** work does not exist yet — define frame → build/freeze Evaluator (show it failing) → Maker restates done-rule → generate / judge / revise or stop.
- **Entry B (retrofit):** work already exists — declare the frame you would hold it to now → freeze rubric before defect-revealing reads → read / score as licensed → exit.
- **Teaching frame:** `../DOCTRINE.md`. **Job form:** the operating instrument sheet.

E-DI does **not** set the frame’s values; it makes quality survive inside a frame the Owner ratifies and the Architect writes.

---

## 4. How does E-DI succeed?

1. **Every role and every iteration stay aligned to the same frame** — done/good, ground truth, and thresholds fixed before the run; misalignment found **before** acceptance (Mandate **M1**, **M2**).
2. **Evaluation is consistent and traceable** — same standard applied the same way; instrument (rubric, Evaluator identity, ground-truth version, measured spread) retained with the artifact (Mandate **M2**; Contracts §10).
3. **Every role receives input that is timely, aware, and actionable** — shortfalls described with class and observation; remedies only where there is real insight (never fabricated); influence channel exists; feedback while work can still change (Mandate **M3**, **M5.5**; **M4** resourceful Maker).

**Underlying mechanisms (Mandate §2):** M1 early identification · M2 consistent evaluation · M3 actionable feedback · M4 resourceful generation · M5 well-managed iteration · plus M6 escalation without interrogation · M7 proportionality · M8 operating level.

**Failure signals (Mandate §4):** ship with no Buyer in the record (F1); judgments cost more than errors caught (F2); scored “good” later rejected by named Buyer/Specialist with no attributable dimension (F3); equivalent implementations disagree beyond measured spread (F4); Owner asked discoverable questions (F5); accepted work not reconstructible from records (F6).

## 6. Who does what in E-DI?

Seats are **roles on a job**, not org-chart titles. One person may hold several seats; double-hats are **declared** on the sheet.

| Seat | Does |
|------|------|
| **Owner** | Like a **Product Manager**: decides **which aspects of the Buyer’s needs** the product will address, and **how the Specialist’s craft** will be expressed through the product. Ratifies the frame; sets values / priority / risk appetite; authorizes dimensions; may override via a **disposition beside** the verdict (never by rewriting the verdict) |
| **Architect** | Converts understanding of the **Buyer’s needs** and the **Owner’s values, priorities, and risk appetites** into a coherent set of descriptions covering **quality attributes, architecture, sequencing, evaluation standards, and thresholds** |
| **Buyer** | Defines the **jobs-to-be-done** by the project’s outputs, the **pain points** to be addressed, and the **gains** to be achieved using those outputs; what “done” means **in their words** (fitness-for-purpose) |
| **Specialist** | The **subject-matter expert** in the field. Understands the craft and can describe what it means to be an **excellent representation** of the craft |
| **Maker** | Receives input and turns it into outputs that are **fit-for-purpose**; takes input and critiques and turns them into **improvements** to the outputs; never judges its own work |
| **Evaluator** | Judges outputs against **defined standards**; clearly describes **shortfalls**; may provide **insights for improvement** — **without ever fabricating** verdicts or suggested remedies |
| **Orchestrator** | Runs the iterative loop; enforces bounds; routes escalations to the **appropriate role**; never impersonates the Maker or Evaluator |

**Bar = Buyer ∩ Specialist.** Buyer supplies jobs-to-be-done, pains, and gains; Specialist supplies craft excellence. Owner chooses which of those the product will serve and how craft is expressed. Architect turns Buyer + Owner into coherent standards and structure. The Evaluator holds authority **on loan** from Buyer and Specialist (Mandate **P2**; `../DOCTRINE.md` §2). Missing Buyer or Specialist on the sheet is not optional silence — escalate (Instrument).

**Contracts’ word “principal”** covers Owner and Architect as separate seats (Mandate preamble).

## Authority — by question, not “always loses”

This playbook is the program’s clarity on **purpose, values, behavior, strategy, priorities, and seats**. Skills, jobs, and other projections align *to* it.

It does **not** replace:

- the **Mandate** — frame of the *doctrine corpus* (what E-DI-as-corpus must / must not do; failure conditions)
- the **Contracts** — machine specification when the delegate is a program (states, schemas, pass-rule algebra, NORMATIVE implementer law)
- the **operating instrument** — form of a *single handoff*
- **`../DOCTRINE.md`** — human teaching of the doctrine

Those answer **different questions**. There is no automatic “fix this page” and no automatic “Contracts always win.” **One exception, by Owner decision (2026-11-02): `../DOCTRINE.md` wins any conflict with this playbook or any other file in the repository, and `../AUTHORITY.json` sets the chain below it.**

### Conflict rule

**Disagreement follows `../AUTHORITY.json`.** A higher tier wins and the lower file is the defect. The Playbook and the Mandate share tier 2: a disagreement between them, or with any other file on its tier, is first tested against `../DOCTRINE.md`, and goes to the Owner only if the doctrine does not answer it. Never an automatic rewrite of either side.

- If Owner clarity on purpose, values, priority, or seats has moved, **projections move** (Contracts proposal, Instrument revision, doctrine teaching update) — the playbook is not obliged to shrink to fit yesterday’s machine text.
- If the playbook is mis-copied or overreaches into machine rules (inventing states, schemas, or pass-rule algebra), **fix the playbook** — that is not its job.
- The playbook **must not invent machine rules**. The Contracts **must not redefine Owner-ratified purpose or seats** without an Owner decision.

### Stability rule (Owner)

**The playbook changes least in this repository.** Prefer amending Contracts, Instrument and the doctrine text. A playbook edit is rare, deliberate, and Owner-ratified. Do not “align the playbook down” every time a projection ships.

### Relationship to the authority chain

`../AUTHORITY.json` is the chain of command: the Owner (tier 0), `../DOCTRINE.md` (tier 1), this playbook and the Mandate (tier 2), the Contracts (tier 3), the Operating Instrument (tier 4), the schemas and fixtures (tier 5), and For Machines, the Quickstart and the Example (tier 6). Within a tier each question has one owner file. Mandate remains the frame instrument for the corpus (Contracts §1.7). Playbook remains the clarity spine for the program.
